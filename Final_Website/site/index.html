00 Manifesto

Most product websites show you a picture of the product.
This one lets you walk through it.

Mail Factory is an automation project exploring system architecture, automation engineering, software testing and system integration. It ships as a native Android application with six surfaces — a Dashboard, an Engine of switchable automation modules, a Generator for single and batch output, a local Library, a One Touch connection surface and a precise Settings tree.

Everything you will meet below is the real application, running live inside this page. The world around it — the typography, the 3D, the gradients, the sequences — exists to explain it. The app is the product. The website is the world that presents it.

Definitions — drawn from the product

Plainly stated

What it is
  • A native Android application.The Dashboard states “100% NATIVE / Android App”.
  • An account generator.Single and batch, with pattern and length controls.
  • A local library.Every record is kept on the device and searchable.
  • An engine array.Six switchable engines and three diagnostic tools.
What it is not
  • Not a web service.There is no account to create and nothing to sign into.
  • Not a sync product.“Nothing is uploaded to a server.”
  • Not finished pretending to be finished.Locked themes, the translation note and the automation caveat are all shown as-is.
  • Not a demo of itself.The application embedded in this page is the real build, version 1.0.0.

06 Library

    
Everything you generate stays here.

    

      
The Library is the archive of the product: All Generated Accounts, marked LIVE, with a running total. Each entry is a SINGLE record or a BATCH, stamped with date and time, carrying its own copy controls for the email and the password.

      
It is deliberately quiet. No cloud, no sync indicator, no account. A record is a thing on your device, and the app says so: “Nothing is uploaded to a server.”

24 Telemetry

      
Four numbers around a core.

      
One Touch shows PING in milliseconds, DOWN and UP in megabytes per second, and a SESSION clock. Before you connect they read “--” and 00:00. The default country is the United States; a country sheet lists the rest.

      
Every explicit navigation into One Touch opens a fresh, disconnected view — the app resets the simulated session on entry and on exit.
